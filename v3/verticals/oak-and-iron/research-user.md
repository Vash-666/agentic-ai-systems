# Oak & Iron Coffee — User Personas

**Research Date:** 2026-09-17  
**Project:** One-page marketing website  
**Location:** Cincinnati, OH

---

## Persona 1: Primary — The Remote Worker

### Profile
- **Name:** Marcus Chen
- **Age:** 29
- **Occupation:** Software Developer (remote)
- **Location:** Northside, Cincinnati
- **Work Style:** Works from home 3 days/week, seeks outside workspace 2 days/week

### Goals When Visiting the Website
1. Quickly determine if the space is laptop-friendly (Wi-Fi, outlets, seating)
2. Check hours to plan his workday (opens early? stays open late?)
3. See if the environment is quiet enough for video calls
4. Find location and parking info
5. Gauge whether he can stay for 3-4 hours without feeling unwelcome

### Pain Points
- Arrives at a coffee shop to find no available seating, or seating with no outlets
- Unclear Wi-Fi policy or unreliable connection
- Too loud for video calls — music or crowds make meetings impossible
- Feels pressured to leave after finishing one drink
- Shops that look good online but feel too cramped or corporate in person
- Can’t tell from the website whether the space is genuinely work-friendly or just a grab-and-go counter

### What Marcus Needs to See on the Site
- **Clear workspace indicators:** "Laptop friendly," photos showing tables with outlets, people working comfortably
- **Hours prominently displayed** — especially opening time and whether they stay open past 5 PM
- **Wi-Fi info:** Explicit mention of free, reliable Wi-Fi
- **Seating variety:** Photos showing a mix of communal tables, individual spots, and quieter corners
- **Location + parking:** Address, neighborhood context (Northside? OTR?), parking situation
- **Menu highlights with prices:** So he can budget for a day of coffee and maybe a pastry
- **Email signup for a loyalty perk:** "First coffee free" or "Remote worker Wednesday discount" would hook him

## Persona 2: Secondary — The Coffee Enthusiast

### Profile
- **Name:** Danielle "Dani" Robinson
- **Age:** 34
- **Occupation:** Graphic Designer
- **Location:** Oakley, Cincinnati
- **Coffee Style:** Owns a V60, a Chemex, and an espresso machine at home. Follows local roasters on Instagram. Reads Sprudge.

### Goals When Visiting the Website
1. Learn about the roasting process and bean sourcing
2. Discover what makes Oak & Iron different from Deeper Roots or Coffee Emporium
3. See the menu — single origins? pour-over options? seasonal drinks?
4. Check if they sell whole beans for home brewing
5. Gauge authenticity: is this a genuine quality shop or just aesthetic?

### Pain Points
- Shops that look industrial and artisanal online but serve mediocre, inconsistent coffee
- No transparency about where beans come from or how they’re roasted
- Menu hidden or unavailable on the website
- Can’t tell if the baristas are knowledgeable or just making lattes
- Generic "coffee shop" vibe with no unique story or craft focus
- No option to buy beans or learn about origins before visiting

### What Dani Needs to See on the Site
- **Hero section with the brand story:** Veteran-owned, craft-focused, locally roasted — the "why" behind the name
- **Roasting process content:** Even a single photo or paragraph about small-batch roasting builds trust
- **Menu highlights with craft details:** Mention of single-origin offerings, pour-over, espresso program, seasonal specials (e.g., salted maple latte)
- **Bean sourcing transparency:** Origin regions, direct trade or ethical sourcing notes
- **Whole bean purchasing info:** Can she buy a bag to take home?
- **Photos that show the bar setup:** Equipment, roasting area, barista craft — not just the seating area
- **Social proof or press:** Any mentions, awards, or enthusiast reviews

## Persona 3: Tertiary — The Tourist

### Profile
- **Name:** Greg Holloway
- **Age:** 42
- **Occupation:** Marketing Manager
- **Location:** Visiting from Chicago for a long weekend
- **Trip Context:** Staying in Downtown/OTR area, exploring Cincinnati’s food and drink scene

### Goals When Visiting the Website
1. Confirm it’s a real local spot, not a generic chain
2. Check location relative to where he’s staying or visiting (Findlay Market, OTR)
3. See hours so he can plan a morning stop before sightseeing
4. Get a feel for the vibe — is it worth prioritizing over other Cincinnati coffee spots?
5. Find out if there’s anything uniquely Cincinnati about it

### Pain Points
- Unclear address or neighborhood context — can’t tell if it’s near his hotel or itinerary
- No hours listed, or hours buried on the site
- Website looks generic or outdated, making the shop seem sketchy or closed
- Can’t tell if it’s a true local gem or a tourist trap
- No sense of the atmosphere — is it sit-down, grab-and-go, or somewhere in between?
- Difficult to navigate on mobile while walking around the city

### What Greg Needs to See on the Site
- **Neighborhood context immediately:** "Located in [Neighborhood], Cincinnati" — ideally with a mention of proximity to landmarks (Findlay Market, Washington Park, etc.)
- **Hours front and center:** Tourists plan mornings around coffee
- **Strong hero imagery:** Industrial/rustic aesthetic that screams "local craft" and makes him want to Instagram it
- **One-sentence differentiator:** Why Oak & Iron? (Veteran-owned, small-batch roasted, community hub — pick the hook)
- **Mobile-first experience:** Large tap targets, fast loading, no pinch-to-zoom required
- **Map or clear address** with parking/transit notes if relevant
- **Menu preview:** A few signature drinks or local favorites so he knows what to order
- **Email signup with a local incentive:** "Get our Cincinnati coffee guide" or "First drink on us" — captures him even as a one-time visitor

## Cross-Persona Insights

| Need | Remote Worker | Coffee Enthusiast | Tourist |
|------|--------------|-------------------|---------|
| **Hours** | Critical — plan workday | Nice to know | Critical — plan itinerary |
| **Location** | Needs parking/neighborhood | Less important | Critical — proximity to hotel/attractions |
| **Menu** | Wants prices + food options | Wants craft details + origins | Wants signature highlights |
| **Work-friendliness** | Top priority | Neutral | Low priority |
| **Brand story** | Nice if it builds trust | Critical for authenticity | Helps confirm "local gem" status |
| **Mobile experience** | Important | Important | **Critical** — often searching while out |
| **Email signup appeal** | Loyalty/discount perk | Coffee origins newsletter | Local guide or first-drink offer |

---

## Design Implications for the One-Page Site

1. **Hero must communicate three things in 3 seconds:** Local Cincinnati coffee shop, industrial/rustic vibe, quality-obsessed craft.
2. **Hours and location should be in the top 20% of the page** — every persona needs this.
3. **Workspace cues (Wi-Fi, outlets, seating)** should be visible without scrolling too far — this is the primary differentiator for the largest audience segment.
4. **Brand story section** should speak to both Dani (authenticity/craft) and Greg (local uniqueness).
5. **Menu highlights** need to balance practical info (prices, food) with craft credibility (origins, roasting).
6. **Email capture** should offer persona-relevant incentives or a generic strong offer (e.g., "Join the crew — get a free drink on your first visit").
7. **Mobile performance is non-negotiable** — tourists and remote workers alike will browse on phones.
